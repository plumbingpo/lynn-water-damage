Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Lynn

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

For homeowners in Lynn, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Coverage

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • How many rooms the water reached, including any ceiling below an upstairs laundry.
  • If the flooring and cabinets have to come out or can be dried where they are.
  • The appliance involved, and if the leak was a slow seep or a fast flood.
