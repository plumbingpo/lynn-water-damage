Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Lynn

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Stay Off the RoofA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • Shingles that are cracked, curled or blown off by wind, exposing the underlayment.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
  • A valley liner that has worn through at the point where the most runoff passes.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Roof Framing

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Rot in these members weakens the structure that holds the roof up.

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Inside the Walls

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. Often the one clue is paint that blisters or trim that swells at the floor.

Drywall Ceilings

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • How long the roof leaked before it was found, and any mold growth.
  • The ceiling texture and paint that the repair has to match.
  • Drywall removal: how much of the ceiling and walls is beyond saving.
  • How much attic insulation is wet and whether it has to be removed.
